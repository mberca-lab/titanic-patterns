What this study can—and cannot—show

Predicting an outcome is not explaining it.

A model can predict recorded outcomes accurately without establishing why they occurred. This study examines patterns, predictive performance, and uncertainty in historical passenger records. Those are different analytical questions, and their answers should not be treated as interchangeable. The findings remain specific to this incomplete historical sample.

A separate, explicitly synthetic promotion example explores how selected audit methods behave under specified assumptions, including deliberately introduced disparities. It illustrates the methods—not a conclusion about real employees or organizations.

04 / Compare group diagnostics

One model. Several diagnostics.

Apply an illustrative fixed threshold of 0.5 to out-of-fold mean probabilities from the reference model. A “positive” means predicted survival, not a recommendation to select or rescue anyone. These descriptive checks answer different questions; cells with a denominator below 20 are suppressed.

The four-fifths rule in employment. US federal selection guidelines (1978) use a selection rate below 80% of the highest group’s as an indicator of possible adverse impact. Here the ratio is descriptive; an employment heuristic does not establish discrimination or fairness in historical survival data. This is not a legal assessment.
Different criteria, different questions. Calibration, predictive parity and equal error rates are different properties. Formal incompatibility results rely on specified definitions and assumptions (Kleinberg, Mullainathan & Raghavan, 2016; Chouldechova, 2017). The mean ratios here do not establish calibration across the probability range.

Checklist, not legal advice

An audit memo in eight lines

  1. Ask what the label measures.Separate recorded outcomes, human assessments and decisions; investigate how each was produced.
  2. Describe before you predict.Outcome rates by group, with denominators and intervals.
  3. Check who is missing.Uneven missingness is a risk to investigate, not proof of unfairness by itself.
  4. Test proxies directly.Can the other columns predict the protected attribute? Report that AUC.
  5. Don’t remove the attribute and declare victory.Measure how much of the gap a blind model still reproduces.
  6. Report rate ratios with intervals.Explain the reference group and use employment heuristics only in an appropriate decision context.
  7. Check calibration and error rates per group.Do not mistake agreement in averages for calibration across the probability range.
  8. Suppress small cells.Here, any rate with a denominator below 20 is not shown.

    07 / Reproduce and limits

    Open the work. Know its limits.

    R and Python share every split and fold. A parity script checks that the Bayesian fits and audit tables agree; the random forests are excluded because their implementations differ. This page bundles the committed result tables and makes no network requests.

    What this cannot tell us

    • Passengers only. . .
    • Recorded sex is binary in the source. These are recorded categories, not identities.
    • Ages are missing for of passengers, unevenly by class.
    • Associations, not causes. Nothing here explains why any individual lived or died.
    • Synthetic means synthetic. The promotion data shows mechanics, not any real organisation.
    • Context, not legal advice. The four-fifths rule and impossibility results frame the audit; they do not settle it.
